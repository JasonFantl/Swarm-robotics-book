# Lightcone Commons — Research Notes & Application Strategy

## What Lightcone Commons is

A brand-new (2026) grantmaking platform run by **Lightcone Infrastructure** — the team behind LessWrong, the Lighthaven campus in Berkeley, and Lightspeed Grants. Their software also ran the S-process that distributed $100M+ through the Survival and Flourishing Fund. It is "a platform for coordinating ambitious philanthropy": many funders pool over one application pipeline, evaluators write public-ish evaluations, and an algorithm splits costs among funders who want the same project (solving the funder free-rider problem).

- **Round 1 deadline: August 23, 2026. Recommendations: ~October 23, 2026.** Rounds repeat every 3 months; applications are living documents you can edit after submitting.
- Expected distribution: **$15M–25M per round**. Grants of any size are culturally acceptable (Lightspeed explicitly welcomed $5k–$5M).
- No 501(c)(3) required; individuals are fine.
- Applications can be imported from LTFF, Manifund, and Grantmaking.ai.

## The people

**Running it:** Oliver Habryka (CEO, Lightcone Infrastructure; habryka@lightconeinfrastructure.com). Known for extremely blunt, high-integrity takes on grantmaking; allergic to spin and grant-speak.

**Round 1 funders:** Jaan Tallinn ($10M conditional), Dustin Moskovitz (~$5M), two anonymous donors ($3M+, $2M+), Long Term Future Fund (~$2M), ARM Fund (~$2M), Timothy Telleen-Lawton, Andreas Stuhlmüller. Priorities vary by funder, but the center of mass is clearly **AI existential risk / long-term future**, with some cause-agnostic room.

**Evaluators (your actual readers):** Zvi Mowshowitz, Eliezer Yudkowsky, Nate Soares, Caleb Parikh (LTFF / AI Risk Mitigation Fund), Elizabeth Van Nostrand (Aceso Under Glass — famous for epistemic-rigor reviews), Oliver Habryka, Yafah Edelman (Epoch AI); Katja Grace in future rounds. Evaluators are paid 2% of grants they direct, so they have skin in the game to find things they genuinely believe in.

## What they want in an application (from Lightspeed Grants, same team)

- "Spell out some basic information about your project in a plain way." Target 1–2 hours of writing, not a polished essay. **Minimalism is a stated value.**
- Rough budget breakdowns, including salary and self-employment tax specifics.
- Update or withdraw promptly if you get funded elsewhere → **disclose the Emergent Ventures and Sloan applications**.
- Lightspeed focus areas: AI x-risk, biosecurity, improving reasoning of decision-makers, crucial considerations — but "applications outside these focus areas are still welcome" and they expected to fund several such.
- The form's own hints: say what changes at different funding levels; name specific people with links and concrete achievements; state urgency.

## How this audience differs from Tyler Cowen / Emergent Ventures

| Emergent Ventures | Lightcone Commons |
|---|---|
| One reader (Tyler), values talent bets, ambition, "moonshots", economics framing | Panel of rationalist evaluators, value calibrated claims, epistemic honesty, concrete models |
| Rewards a great hook and confident narrative | Punishes hype; rewards plainly stating uncertainty and what you don't know |
| Cause-agnostic | Center of mass is AI x-risk / long-term future; cause-agnostic at the margin |
| Fixed essay, tweet-length pitch | Four plain questions, living document, tiered budget expected |

## Strategy for this application

1. **Do not pretend this is an AI safety project.** These evaluators detect and heavily penalize motivated reframing. State plainly that this is a field-building/education project in decentralized multi-agent systems, and let them decide.
2. **But do state the genuine adjacencies, calibrated:** (a) multi-agent emergent behavior is background knowledge increasingly relevant to reasoning about multi-agent AI systems; (b) your personal track record — leaving Anduril over autonomous-weapons concerns, the Goodfire AI-safety fellowship, the planned "The Real World" ethics chapter — is real evidence about who they'd be funding.
3. **Lean on the distillation/explorable-explanations culture.** LessWrong deeply values technical distillation (3Blue1Brown, Nicky Case's explorable explanations, Distill.pub). "There is no great explainer for this field, and I have a 6-year public track record of making exactly these" is a strong, verifiable claim to this audience.
4. **Tiered budget.** The form asks what changes at different levels — give ~3 tiers instead of EV's single $25k ask.
5. **Every claim should be checkable via a link.** These evaluators will actually click through to jasonfantl.com and the HN threads.
6. **Urgency framing is honest and useful:** you go full-time in ~August regardless; Round 1 money arrives late October and determines how long the full-time runway lasts.
